## The speedup, re-measured

`sha256()` over a rope, 7 alternating runs per side, ms, median (range). 10 MB is the generator of `tuning/bench/kb.c`; 50 KB is the mean of 200 calls per run. Raw lines in `bench.log`.

| text | compiler | before | after | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 MB | gcc -O2 | 84.0 (83.0 to 84.8) | 39.2 (38.1 to 39.8) | 2.14 |
| 10 MB | clang -O2 | 77.8 (75.9 to 87.7) | 41.0 (40.3 to 44.7) | 1.90 |
| 50 KB | gcc -O2 | 0.448 (0.400 to 0.497) | 0.186 (0.184 to 0.201) | 2.41 |
| 50 KB | clang -O2 | 0.382 (0.378 to 0.397) | 0.192 (0.188 to 0.209) | 1.99 |

The ranges do not overlap in any row, so the gain is measurable. The PR's "2.1 times" holds for gcc; under clang it is 1.9. Absolute times here are lower than the PR's (113 to 54 ms): a different host, same ratio.

1. The PR body says "before 113 ms (113 to 117)", `hardware-tuning.md` says 124 (113 to 141) for the same build. They are two different sets of runs (5 and 7); both give about 2.1 with the after figure. Nothing to change in the code.
2. The code comment says "2.1 times faster, measured". It is gcc's figure; clang's is 1.9. Fine as it stands.

## Residual risk

The check is against one trusted implementation on one architecture (x86-64, little endian). The code uses only `uint32_t` arithmetic and shifts, no byte-order or word-size assumptions, so a 32-bit or big-endian build should agree (**hypothesis**: not run; no 32-bit libraries here).
