## 3. Findings

### Finding 1 (required): the unit test's frame gate measures wall clock and fails under load

`tests/test_org.c` requires a 64 KB line to be coloured in under 100 ms of `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`. `test_org` run 40 times beside busy loops on the 4 cores, alternating with the same test on `CLOCK_PROCESS_CPUTIME_ID` (`gate-cpu.diff`, two tokens):

| Busy loops | wall clock (the PR) fails | CPU time fails | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 of 40 | 0 of 40 | measured |
| 4 | 0 of 40 | 0 of 40 | measured |
| 8 | 7 of 40 | 0 of 40 | measured |
| 12 | 29 of 40 | 0 of 40 | measured |

With the CPU clock the PR's three frame-gate plants are still caught (measured, each once). A unit test that fails on a busy machine makes a merge train red for a PR that did nothing wrong, and gives the plants that run `test TESTS=org` a catch they did not earn. Fixed in 200af04 (section 6).

### Finding 2 (decision before the keys are bound): a `++` repeater more than 10 intervals overdue stops half way

`small/h.org`: `* WAIT water the plants` with `SCHEDULED: <2026-11-01 Sun ++1d>`, one step to DONE on 2026-11-19. Org asks "10 repeater intervals were not enough to shift date past today. Continue?". The PR does what Org does on "no": the keyword is back at TODO and LAST_REPEAT is written, but the date stays at 2026-11-10 (still past) and no LOGBOOK note is made. Org on "yes" (`yes.el`) gives `<2026-11-20 Fri ++1d>` and the note (measured, 1 run each). Homeros has no question to ask there, and a habit missed for 11 days is ordinary use (FEATURES.md lists habits), so the answer should be chosen, not fall out of batch Emacs. I recommend "yes". Nothing calls `org_todo_step` yet, so this does not block the merge; it should be settled before package 5 binds `cit`. The other places where Org stops with an error (an hour repeater on a stamp without a time, "Not at a timestamp") leave the text as the PR's comment says "as Emacs leaves the text", but the oracle skips every text on which Org stops, so that claim is a hypothesis: 3 of my extra plants sit there and nothing can catch them.

### Finding 3 (minor): space-then-tab indentation

The new LOGBOOK, property drawer and note are indented by `ind()`, which counts every tab as 8 (right for `org-current-text-column`, which the parser needs). Emacs's `current-indentation` goes to the next tab stop, so after ` \t` Org indents to column 8 and the PR to column 9 (`small/a.org`, `small/b.org`; 117 of 2,000 texts with tabs, measured). Indentation by tabs alone, or by spaces alone, agrees (`directed/indent9*`, `indent17`).

### Finding 4 (minor): a date that does not exist with a month or year repeater

`small/e.org`: `SCHEDULED: <2026-02-30 Mon +1m>`. Org adds the month to the digits and then normalises: 2026-03-30. `tschange` normalises first (March 2) and then adds: 2026-04-02 (measured; all 15 differences of the "wide" run are of this kind, and 0 of 3,000 differ when every date exists). Reading year, month and day from the stamp's digits in `tschange`, as `tsbody` already does, fixes it and makes the `civil()` call there unnecessary.

### Finding 5: test gaps in the TODO code

34 extra plants in `org_todo_step`'s code (`mutants.txt`), each run against the unit test (which is what the PR's six TODO plants run), the 500 texts `make check` compares, and 5,000 more (`mutants.log`):

- the unit test catches 2 of 34; the check's 500 texts 14; all 5,500 texts 19 (measured, 1 run);
- of the 15 that survive everything, 6 are caught by one hand-written text each (`survivors.log`): a property whose name only starts with ORDERED (`ordered-prefix`), the end of a time range carried below 0 minutes (`range-restart`), an indent of 8 or more columns (`indent9`), COMMENT after a priority (`prio-comment`), a repeating stamp in a table row (`table`), lower-case planning (`lower-planning`). The generator needs these lines;
- 3 sit on the paths where Org stops (finding 2);
- 6 are not classified: a `/N` repeater suffix, `CLOSED:` inside a word, a LAST_REPEAT name by prefix, the 16-byte limit after a stamp's time, the indent of a note whose parent is a drawer, and the new LOGBOOK moved before a following headline. Each is either a missing text or code that is not needed (hypothesis; the last one also survives the unit test's own case of a LOGBOOK before a child).

### Finding 6: code that could be shorter

`shorter.diff` writes `kwmatch` through the existing `todo()` and `spaces()` and replaces four more hand-written space loops with `spaces()`: 13 lines fewer, the unit test passes and 0 of 5,500 texts differ from Org (measured, 1 run). Smaller copies a reader meets (not measured): `tsbody` repeats `tstime`'s reading of the date, day name and time (they copy two of Org's regexps that differ in the spaces allowed); the three planning keywords are spelled out in `planningl`, `after_planning` and `kwtime`.

### Finding 7 (note): one parse of the entry per repeating stamp

`repeat` calls `agenda_ts` for every repeating stamp, and each call parses the whole entry. One entry of k stamps, WAIT to DONE (`perf2.log`, median of 5): 100 stamps 0.9 ms, 800 stamps 41.5 ms, 3,200 stamps 676 ms. No real entry has hundreds of repeating stamps, so this is a record, not a request; parsing the entry once would remove it.
