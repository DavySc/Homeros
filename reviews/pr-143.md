### Verdict

#### Finding 1 (blocking): what slows plain text too is invisible

Each timed check is now `Org text < bound * plain text`, and the plain text runs through the same `scan_at`, `meta`, `fontify` and `orgfx_conceal`. A slowdown in that shared code multiplies both sides. Main's bounds in milliseconds saw it. My plants (`pr-143-repro/review143.txt`, the project's plant format), each run 10 times idle against the head's test and against main's test with the same plant:

| Plant | head's test | main's test |
|---|---|---|
| X1: every line of a walk scanned five times | passes 10 of 10 | fails 10 of 10 (frame gate) |
| X2: every line coloured five times | passes 10 of 10 | fails 10 of 10 (long-line gate) |
| X3: every line of a walk scanned twice | passes 10 of 10 | fails 1 of 10 |
| X4: `p < eol(l)` for `p < e` in `fontify`'s first loop over the line (the line's end looked for again at every byte; a 64 KB line of plain words takes 60 ms for 5.4) | passes 10 of 10 | fails 10 of 10 (long-line gate) |

With X4 the long-line ratios are 1.14 to 1.86 against a bound of 16: the slower the shared code, the nearer every ratio comes to 1, so the check gets easier to pass as the code gets worse. X4 is the kind of slip the long-line gate exists for (a scan to the line's end from every position); it only happens to sit where plain words reach it too.

Two references that do not run the same code on the same amount of text, measured (`mkalt.py`; clean head and each plant, 198 rounds each over the four load states, least to most):

| Reference | clean | X1 (walk 5x) | X2 (colour 5x) | X4 (quadratic line) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: the plain 64 KB line over a sixteenth of it (4 KB) | 7.7 to 17.8 (40 rounds, beside the build) | same as clean | same as clean | **104 to 184** |
| A': the plain line over a quarter of it | 2.7 to 5.0 | same | same | 10.9 to 24.7 |
| B1: the plain 64 KB line over a byte loop in the test (16 passes counting newlines) | 0.9 to 2.6 | same | **5.9 to 13.3** | **28 to 68** |
| B2: the plain 100,000-line frame over that byte loop | 0.23 to 0.68 | **1.0 to 2.9** | same | same |

- **A** costs one line of test (`colour_line` on the plain line and on its last sixteenth), needs nothing outside the test, cannot depend on the machine (linear code gives at most 16 on any machine), and separates the quadratic plant by 5.8 times. It does not see a constant factor.
- **B** sees all three, a constant factor too, with the 5x plants 1.5 times (X1) and 2.3 times (X2) past the clean maximum. Its ratio compares two different kinds of code, so it may sit elsewhere on another CPU (hypothesis; measured on this machine only).

**Required:** the long-line gate must fail on a slowdown of the plain line itself that grows with its length; reference A does that, and X4 (or one like it) joins `tests/plants/org.txt`. **For the author to decide and say in the PR:** whether to add B for constant factors. Main's bound caught a 5x slowdown here and not a 2x one, and it would have caught neither on a runner twice as fast, so I do not hold the PR to that part; the 2 ms frame gate on Davy's machine (DESIGN.md, phase 0) is where a constant factor is judged.

The many-lines side has no plant of mine: a walk repeated per line is caught by the checkpoint check (plant 91), and I found no realistic slip that grows with the number of lines and escapes it.

#### Finding 2 (blocking): plant 103 escapes, because of the rule "any of 3 tries"

Plant 103 (links searched from the scan's start) slows its two gates by a constant 3 times, not by a growing amount. One try's ratio, over all four load states:

| | gate 1 (bound 3.5) | gate 4 (bound 4) |
|---|---|---|
| clean head, 312 tries | 1.03 to 2.95 | 1.05 to 2.69 |
| plant 103, 68 tries | 2.66 to 6.21 | 4.35 to 8.75 |

On gate 1 the two ranges overlap (the planted code's fastest try is under the clean code's slowest), and the test passes a gate when any one of 3 tries is under the bound. A slow plain-side try (the divisor) is as good as a fast Org-side try, so noise on either side helps a plant through. It got through once in 235 runs; PR 127's review named this plant as the next to escape, and this PR's purpose includes it.

The same recorded tries, taken 3 in a row (`min3.py`; computed from the probe's tries, not run as a test):

| | any of 3 tries (the PR's rule): least ratio | least Org time of 3 over least plain time of 3 |
|---|---|---|
| gate 1 clean (104 groups) | 1.03 to 1.53 | **1.43 to 1.58** |
| gate 1 plant 103 (22 groups) | 3.62 to 4.62 | **4.46 to 4.78** |
| gate 4 clean | 1.05 to 1.74 | **1.66 to 1.81** |
| gate 4 plant 103 | 4.35 to 6.08 | **5.81 to 6.08** |
| gate 6 clean | 12.6 to 21.2 | **16.8 to 22.7** |
| gate 6 plant 133 (22 groups) | 49.2 to 84.4 | **70.7 to 85.8** |
| long line `a[fn:1] ` clean | 6.6 to 9.6 | **9.0 to 9.8** |

Noise only adds time, so the least of 3 on each side is the steady number: the clean spread on gate 1 falls from 2.9 times (one try) to 1.1 times, and clean and planted are 2.8 times apart on gate 1, 3.2 on gate 4 and 3.1 on gate 6. The bounds can then sit in the middle of a real gap. The cost is that all 3 tries always run (the timed part of `test_org` about 3 times as long; the whole test took 1.2 s here).

**Required:** each timed check compares the least of its 3 Org times with the least of its 3 plain times (or another rule under which plant 103 does not escape in a few hundred runs), with the bounds set again from the new numbers.

### 3. Any test-only hooks in production code?

None (read). The diff touches `src/org/org.c` on one line: `cover()` loses `z < r[0] ||`.

- **Proved by reading:** `r` starts as `{ l, l }` in `fn_line`; `r[0]` only ever becomes a span's start `a`, and every `a` is the line's start, a definition's line above, or a reference's position on this line. Every `z` handed to `cover()` is past the current reference or past `l`, and references are visited left to right. So `z > r[0]` always and the dropped test could not be true.
- **Measured:** 0 of 20,000 random texts differ between the head's and main's -O2 drivers in three modes (`memo_fuzz.py` of PR 118's review, seeds 1, 2, 11, 12), and a build that traps when `z < r[0]` gave the same output as the head on 20,000 more (seeds 21, 22) and passed `test_org`.
