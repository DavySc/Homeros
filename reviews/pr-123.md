## What the plant needs (measured)

The plant `loop: win: proc_wait waits on the other children` shows only if `say` (child a) has exited before proc_wait's first wait: its process handle is then signalled in every wait, the alertable wait returns at once, and the write's completion routine, which closes slowcat's stdin, never runs (**hypothesis** for the mechanism; the instrumented runs below agree with it). So the old `Sleep(100)` missed whenever `say` took more than 100 ms to start and exit.

`reviews/pr-123-repro/win.sh` builds `waits()` alone (the other tests cut from `main`) against the clean and the planted `src/loop/`, and runs the two alternately. "Caught" below is strict: the planted run failed at `CHECK(proc_wait(id, 3000) == 0)`. 4 cores, Wine from tools.nix, `make check` running beside it throughout.

## Findings

### 1. The unplanted test still fails under heavy load: `say` can finish before its reads are armed (blocking, one line)

**Measured**: 5 of 130 clean runs of the PR's test failed with 12 busy loops beside it (main's test: 3 of 30), all at

    tests/test_loop_win.c:513: CHECK(!a.nout && !a.ended[1] && !a.ended[0] && !fired[4]) failed

An instrumented copy (60 runs per side, 7 bad runs) printed the same thing in every bad run: after `loop_once(0)`, `a.nout == 6`, `a.ended[1]` and `a.ended[0]` were already set, and the mutex wait returned WAIT_ABANDONED after 0 ms. The mutex is created unowned, so `say` takes it at once, writes and exits while `slowcat` is still being started; `loop_once(0)` then reads its output and reports its exit before proc_wait begins. On a clean tree that is a false red. On a planted tree the run still fails, at that CHECK instead of the plant's (6 of 130; seen in the 3 of the last 100 where I recorded it), so planted.sh would report "caught" for the wrong reason.

This race is older than the PR, but the PR's mutex closes it for one word and one statement: create the mutex owned and release it only after the reads are armed (`reviews/pr-123-repro/owned.diff`):

    -	HANDLE m = CreateMutexA(NULL, FALSE, "homeros-say");
    +	HANDLE m = CreateMutexA(NULL, TRUE, "homeros-say");   /* a says nothing before its reads are armed */
    ...
    -	while (WaitForSingleObject(m, 5000) == WAIT_OBJECT_0) { ReleaseMutex(m); Sleep(1); }
    +	do { ReleaseMutex(m); Sleep(1); } while (WaitForSingleObject(m, INFINITE) != WAIT_ABANDONED);

**Measured** under the same load: 75 of 75 clean passes, 75 of 75 planted runs failed at proc_wait. No line more than the PR has. It also removes the PR's silent fall-through: as written, a WAIT_TIMEOUT after 5 s ends the loop and the test goes on as if `say` had exited; with the variant a `say` that never exits hangs the test into check.sh's 120 s limit, which is a loud failure.

### 2. Two runs in one Wine prefix (or one Windows session) now always fail (non-blocking, the author's or thread 0's call)

The mutex has one fixed name, and named objects are shared by every process of a Wine server (on Windows, of a session). **Measured**, two copies of `waits()` started together in one prefix, 20 pairs: main's test 0 of 40 failed, the PR's 40 of 40 (at `CHECK(ms() < end)` in `run` and at the last `proc_wait(id, 1000)` CHECK), the variant of finding 1 40 of 40. Nothing in the repo does this today (`make win` runs the checks one after the other and planted.sh gives every plant its own copy and so its own prefix), which is why it does not block. A name of the run's own, handed to the child in the environment, costs three lines (`reviews/pr-123-repro/pid.diff`): 0 of 40 failed two at once, and 30 of 30 clean, 30 of 30 caught under load.

### 3. On Windows itself the plant is never caught, with either test (not this PR's; for package 10)

`test_loop_win` runs only under Wine in CI. I ran it on a `windows-latest` runner through a temporary workflow on the review branch (deleted again in the report commit): runs 45040488827 and 45043401481.

- `waits()` on the clean tree: PR 123 passed 60 of 60 (20 of them with 4 busy loops), main's test 59 of 60, the variant of finding 1 20 of 20, the variant with finding 2's name 17 of 20 (**measured**). So the change itself is sound on Windows.
- The planted tree: the plant's CHECK failed in 0 of 60 runs with the PR's test, 0 of 60 with main's, 0 of 20 with each variant; the planted program exits 0. The plant's bug shows through a behaviour of Wine's alertable wait that Windows does not share (**hypothesis**), so this plant proves the check only under Wine.
- Every failure on the clean tree there was an older timing check, not the mutex: `CHECK(ms() - t0 >= 190 && ms() - t0 < 700)` after `proc_wait(id, 200)` (main's test 1 of 60, finding 2's variant 3 of 20, the others 0 of 80; it also failed in 5 planted runs), and `CHECK(!fired[0] && ms() - t0 >= 195)` at line 309 failed the first of 6 runs of the whole test. GetTickCount64 steps by about 16 ms there, so a 200 ms wait can read as 187 depending on where in a step it starts (**hypothesis**; it would also explain why the variant that does more before that check is hit more often).

None of this is caused by PR 123; it matters the day test_loop_win joins the Windows job.
